# Brand editorial guidelines

Source of truth for GenStudio **Brand → Editorial guidelines** and **Brand → Editorial restrictions**. Do not repeat these lines in the prompt when Brand is selected in Parameters.

Approved short names (what you may shorten *to*, and what never to use) stay in Product guidelines: [products.md](products.md).

Email body rhythm and character limits stay in Channel guidelines: [channel-guidelines.md](channel-guidelines.md).

## Configured in Brand → Editorial guidelines

- Expand abbreviations on first use, followed by the abbreviation in parentheses: “A large language model (LLM) is a type of artificial intelligence.”
- Refer to products by full name on first use, after first use of the full name, you may use approved abbreviations. For example “Red Hat Enterprise Linux is the full product name and the abbreviation is RHEL”
- Use parentheses only for approved initialisms such as RHEL; for other products, shorten to the approved name without inventing an initialism.
- Leave common terms such as AI, HTML, and PIN abbreviated; they do not need expansion on first use.
- Use sentence case for headlines and sub-headlines: capitalize only the first word, plus product names and proper nouns.
- Use “application,” not “app.”
- Use numerals for numbers, including those under 10, except when a number begins a sentence.
- Refer to Red Hat as “it,” not “they.” Keep “Red Hat” on one line.

## Configured in Brand → Editorial restrictions

- Avoid absolute and superlative claims such as leading, best, fastest, only, or 100% unless a citation appears in the copy.
- Avoid calling a product or environment “secure” or “more secure.” Name the capability or outcome instead (for example, compliance baselines, live kernel patching).
- Avoid AI-typical wording: delve, harness, pivotal, landscape, unwavering, and openings like “In today’s fast-paced…”
- Avoid vague words used alone: robust, powerful, strong, streamline, leverage, utilize, seamless, frictionless, enhance, improve.
- Avoid “the” before product names (write “Red Hat Enterprise Linux,” not “the Red Hat Enterprise Linux”).
- Avoid “please” and “click here.”

## How this layers

| Layer | Owns |
|-------|------|
| **Brand editorial** | First-use pattern, headline case, numerals, application vs app, Red Hat as “it” |
| **Brand restrictions** | Absolutes, security claims, AI-typical wording, vague words, “the” before products, please / click here |
| **Product** | Which short forms are approved (RHEL, OpenShift Platform Plus, Ansible Automation Platform, product trial) and which are forbidden (EL, OPP, AAP, free trial) |
| **Channel (email)** | Per-field General, Subject, Preheader, Headline, Sub-headline, Body, CTA (2–5 each); contractions allowed; ban “flexible” / “scalable”; ban “free” / “win” / “unlock” (use no-cost) |

Do not add trademark symbols (® / ™ / r-ball) to Brand or Channel guidelines. Preserve marks only when the user supplies them in a brief.
